## What are Semantic Elements?
Semantic elements clearly describe their meaning to both the browser and the developer. Examples include `<header>`, `<footer>`, `<article>`, `<section>`, `<nav>`, and `<main>`.

## Why Use Semantic HTML?
- Improves accessibility for screen readers
- Helps search engines understand page structure
- Makes code easier to read and maintain

## Common Semantic Tags
- `<header>` — top section of a page or article
- `<nav>` — navigation links
- `<main>` — main content of the page
- `<section>` — a grouped section of content
- `<article>` — independent self-contained content
- `<aside>` — side content like a sidebar
- `<footer>` — bottom section of a page

## Non-Semantic vs Semantic
Non-semantic: `<div>`, `<span>` — tell nothing about content
Semantic: `<article>`, `<nav>` — clearly describe content
